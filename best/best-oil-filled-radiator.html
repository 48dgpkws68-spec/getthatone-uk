Buying guide: how to choose the right oil filled radiators UK

Is an oil-filled radiator cheap to run?

Every plug-in electric heater turns nearly all its electricity into heat, so a 2 kW oil radiator uses the same energy per hour on full power as a 2 kW fan heater. The difference is in how it delivers that heat. The oil stores warmth, so once the room is warm the thermostat switches the element off for longer periods while the fins keep radiating. A good thermostat and timer, and heating only the room you are in, are what keep the bill down.

How much power do you need?

As a rough guide, allow about 100 W per square metre in a reasonably insulated UK room. A 1.5 kW radiator suits a bedroom or box room up to about 15 square metres, 2 kW suits most living rooms up to about 20 to 25 square metres and 2.5 kW is for larger or draughty rooms. More fins usually means more surface area and steadier heat.

Thermostat and timer

A thermostat is essential: it stops the radiator running flat out once the room is warm. Electronic thermostats with a display in degrees are more precise than simple numbered dials. A timer lets the radiator switch on before you get up or come home and switch off when the room is empty, which is often the biggest single saving.

Smart radiators: worth it?

Smart models with an app let you set weekly schedules, change the temperature from your phone and, on some models, use Alexa or Google Home. That is useful if your days vary or you want the room warm before you arrive. Most budget smart radiators only connect to 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi, so check your router before you buy.

Safety features to look for

Choose a radiator with a tip-over switch and overheat protection. Never cover it or dry clothes on it, keep it away from curtains and furniture and plug it straight into a wall socket rather than an extension lead, because a 2 kW or 2.5 kW heater draws close to the limit of a standard UK 13 A plug. A child lock is useful in family homes.

Oil-filled or oil-free?

Oil-free radiators, such as Dimplex's Eco range, use a different internal material and are lighter and quicker to warm up. Oil-filled radiators are heavier and slower, but store more heat and keep radiating for longer after switching off. For long, steady heating in one room, an oil-filled radiator is usually the more comfortable choice.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best oil filled radiator in the UK?

For most homes the De'Longhi Dragon 4 is the best choice, thanks to its even heating, precise thermostat and 24 hour timer. The Russell Hobbs RHOFR5002 is the best budget option and the Mill Gentle Air WiFi the best premium radiator.

How much does it cost to run a 2kW oil filled radiator?

On full power a 2 kW radiator uses 2 kWh per hour. Multiply that by your electricity unit rate to get the hourly cost at full power. In practice the thermostat switches it off for part of the time once the room is warm, so the real cost is usually well below that maximum.

Can you leave an oil filled radiator on overnight?

Many people do, and models with a tip-over switch, overheat protection and thermostat are designed for long use. Keep it clear of bedding and curtains, use a low setting and a timer, and plug it directly into a wall socket rather than an extension lead.

Do oil filled radiators need refilling?

No. The oil is sealed inside at the factory and never needs topping up or replacing. If a radiator ever leaks oil, stop using it and contact the manufacturer.

Are oil filled radiators better than fan heaters?

They are better for steady, silent heat over several hours, for example in a bedroom or living room in the evening. A fan heater warms a small space faster and suits short bursts, such as a quick blast in the bathroom.

Why does my oil filled radiator smell when first used?

A slight smell during the first hours of use is normal and comes from manufacturing residue burning off the element and fins. Run it on full power in a ventilated room for an hour or two and the smell should disappear.

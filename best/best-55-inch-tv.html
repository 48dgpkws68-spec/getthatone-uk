Buying guide: how to choose the right 55 inch TVs

OLED, QD-OLED or Mini LED?

OLED and QD-OLED switch off each pixel individually, so blacks are perfect and the picture looks great from any angle. They are ideal for films and evening viewing. Mini LED TVs use thousands of tiny backlights and get much brighter, which helps in sunny rooms and with daytime sport, and they carry no risk of burn-in. A basic QLED or LED set is fine for casual viewing but struggles with dark scenes.

Which HDR formats matter?

Nearly every 4K TV supports HDR10 and HLG, which is what BBC iPlayer uses for its UHD content. Netflix and Disney+ use Dolby Vision for most of their HDR titles, while Prime Video favours HDR10+. LG and Sony support Dolby Vision, Samsung only HDR10+, and Hisense, TCL and Philips support both. If you stream a lot of Netflix, Dolby Vision is worth having.

Gaming: HDMI 2.1 and 120 Hz

A PS5 or Xbox Series X can output 4K at 120 frames per second, but only through an HDMI 2.1 port on a 120 Hz or faster TV. Look for VRR (variable refresh rate) and ALLM (auto low latency mode) too. Check how many HDMI 2.1 ports a TV has if you also connect a soundbar via eARC, because that can use up one of them.

Freeview Play and UK apps

All TVs in this list include Freeview Play, so you get the live channels from your aerial plus BBC iPlayer, ITVX, Channel 4 and My5 in one guide. webOS, Tizen and Google TV have the widest app choice. VIDAA and Titan OS cover the big services but have fewer smaller apps. If you rely on a niche app, check it is available before you buy.

Sound and size

Thin TVs have small speakers, so dialogue can sound thin. Sony and TCL do better than most, but a soundbar of £150 to £300 makes a bigger difference than paying extra for a pricier TV. As for size: a 55 inch 4K TV looks sharp from about 1.5 to 2.5 metres away, which suits most UK lounges.

When to buy

New models launch in spring, and last year's TVs drop in price over the summer and again around Black Friday and Boxing Day. A 2025 model at a 2026 price is often the smartest buy, as the year-on-year improvements are usually small. Most TVs on Amazon.co.uk come with a manufacturer's warranty of at least one year.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best 55 inch TV in the UK right now?

For most people the LG C5 (OLED55C54LA) is the best 55 inch TV: excellent contrast, four HDMI 2.1 ports and all UK apps for around £1,050. The Samsung S90F is the best value OLED at around £799.

Is OLED worth the extra money?

If you watch a lot of films or TV in the evening, yes. OLED gives perfect blacks and wide viewing angles. In a very bright room a Mini LED such as the Samsung QN90F can be the better choice.

Can OLED TVs get burn-in?

Modern OLEDs use pixel shifting and refresh cycles to reduce the risk, and for normal mixed viewing it is rarely a problem. If you leave a static channel logo or game HUD on screen for many hours a day, a Mini LED is the safer option.

How far should I sit from a 55 inch TV?

Around 1.5 to 2.5 metres is ideal for a 55 inch 4K TV. Closer than that you may notice the edges of the screen, further away and a 65 inch could make more sense.

Do I need a TV licence?

Yes, in the UK you need a TV licence to watch or record live TV on any channel or to use BBC iPlayer, whichever TV you buy. You do not need one if you only stream services such as Netflix or Disney+.

Which 55 inch TV is best for a PS5?

The LG C5, LG G5 and Samsung S90F all have four HDMI 2.1 ports with 4K at 120 Hz or more, VRR and very low input lag. On a budget the Hisense U7Q is a good choice with 165 Hz support.

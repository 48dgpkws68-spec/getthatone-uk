Buying guide: how to choose the right 43-inch TV under £300

What does £300 buy in a 43-inch TV?

In 2026 a £300 budget covers most 43-inch 4K TVs on Amazon.co.uk. Between £250 and £300 you get QLED colour, usually with Dolby Vision or HDR10+, from TCL, Hisense, Samsung, Philips and Amazon. Around £190 to £230 you find basic LED sets and the CHiQ QLED, which are fine for a bedroom or kitchen. What you do not get at this size and price is OLED, Mini LED or a reliable 120 Hz panel.

43 inch or 50 inch?

A 43-inch TV is roughly 96 cm wide, about 15 cm narrower than a 50-inch set, which matters on a chest of drawers, in an alcove or on a kitchen wall. If you have the width, bigger is often barely more expensive: our guide to the best TV under £300 lists 50 and 55-inch models at the same budget. If 43 inches is the limit, the ten TVs here are the ones to compare.

QLED or LED?

QLED adds a quantum dot layer for richer reds and greens, and under £300 six of the ten TVs here have it. None of them has local dimming worth mentioning, so dark scenes look a little grey on every set in this guide. A standard LED TV such as the TCL 43V6C-UK, LG 43UA73006LA or Hisense 43E6QTUK is cheaper and fine for the news, soaps and a second room.

Gaming on a 43-inch TV

We list every TV in this guide as a 60 Hz set. That is fine for a Nintendo Switch and for most PS5 and Xbox games at 60 frames per second, but not for 120 Hz modes. Do not be misled by terms such as 120 Hz Game Accelerator in a product title: that is not the native refresh rate. Low latency modes such as ALLM on the LG and Game Mode Plus on the Hisense 43A7QTUK do help.

Dolby Vision, HDR10+ and smart platforms

The TCL 43P7K-UK, Hisense 43A7QTUK and Amazon Omni QLED support Dolby Vision, which Netflix and Disney+ use most. Samsung and Philips support HDR10+ instead, and the LG only HDR10 and HLG. Google TV (TCL) has the biggest app choice, webOS (LG) and Tizen (Samsung) are the most polished, VIDAA (Hisense) includes Freely, Roku TV (CHiQ) is the simplest and Fire TV suits Alexa users. A streaming stick can always replace a weak platform.

Prices move around

TV prices on Amazon.co.uk change often, especially around Black Friday and in January. The Samsung QE43Q7FAAU, the Amazon Omni QLED and at times the TCL 43P7K-UK have all been listed above £300, so they can cross the limit of this guide between sales. Check the current price before you buy. If a pick goes over budget, the Hisense 43A7QTUK gives you a similar QLED picture for around £250.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best 43-inch TV under £300?

The TCL 43P7K-UK. It is a QLED with Dolby Vision, HDR10+ and Google TV, for around £259 on Amazon.co.uk.

Is a 43-inch TV big enough for a living room?

For a viewing distance of roughly 1.2 to 2 metres, yes. If you sit further away and have the width, a 50 or 55-inch TV from our guide to the best TV under £300 is worth considering.

Is 4K worth it on a 43-inch TV?

Yes. All ten TVs in this guide are 4K, and at this size it no longer costs more than Full HD. You see the extra sharpness most when you sit close, as in a bedroom.

Which 43-inch TV under £300 is best for PS5 or Xbox?

The TCL 43P7K-UK has the best picture for games, but like every TV here we count it as 60 Hz, so 120 Hz modes are out. The Amazon Omni QLED has one HDMI 2.1 port with eARC.

What is the cheapest good 43-inch TV?

The TCL 43V6C-UK at around £189. It is a basic LED set, but it has Google TV with all the main UK apps and Chromecast built in. The CHiQ U43QM9K costs about the same and adds QLED colour.

Which 43-inch TV has Freely?

The Hisense 43A7QTUK and the Hisense 43E6QTUK both have Freely built in, which brings live TV and catch-up over the internet without an aerial.

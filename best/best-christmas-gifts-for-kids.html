Buying guide: how to choose the right Christmas gifts for kids

Start with the age on the box

The age a manufacturer prints on a toy is about safety and difficulty. LEGO DUPLO starts at 2, most Playmobil sets at 4 and larger LEGO sets at 8 or 9. A set that is far too hard ends up half built. Choose a toy with an age label that matches the child, or a year or two above it for a child who already builds a lot.

Look past Christmas morning

The best Christmas gift keeps children busy through the school holidays. Building sets and marble runs offer a project for the first days and play after that. Audio players and cameras are used every day. Toys with only one trick tend to be forgotten by New Year.

Set a budget and share the big gifts

Most gifts in this list cost between £20 and £70. A games console costs several hundred pounds, so it often works best as a family present or one shared with grandparents. A cheaper set such as the LEGO City Camper Van makes a good second present under the tree.

Batteries, cards and film

Check what is not in the box. The DUPLO train and the VTech camera need batteries, the Yoto needs story cards and the instax needs film. Buy these extras at the same time so nothing is missing on Christmas Day.

Order in good time

Popular toys sell out in December and delivery slots fill up in the last week before Christmas. Order by early December and check the delivery date on Amazon.co.uk. Keep the receipt in case the child already has the same toy.

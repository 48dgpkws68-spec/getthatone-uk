The 10 best energy efficient electric heaters of 2026

Short answer (reference prices as of 25 November 2026): our top pick is the De'Longhi Dragon 4 TRDX40820E at approx. £149 on Amazon. Best value: Russell Hobbs 2kW Oil Filled Radiator RHOFR5002 at approx. £55. Cheapest in this top 10: Pro Breeze 2000W Mini Ceramic Fan Heater at approx. £29.

With energy prices still high, many UK households heat just the room they are in rather than running the central heating for the whole house. A portable electric heater makes that easy, but there is a lot of confusion about which type is cheapest to run. Every electric heater turns almost all of its electricity into heat; the savings come from a good thermostat, a timer and choosing the right type for your room. We compared the heaters on Amazon.co.uk on exactly those points, on price and on buyer reviews.

How to choose in 5 questions

Will the heater run for hours or in short bursts?

For an evening in the living room, an oil-filled radiator holds heat and cycles off, like No. 1 De'Longhi Dragon 4 at £149 or No. 2 Russell Hobbs at £55. For a quick warm-up in a small room you only use briefly, a ceramic fan heater such as No. 8 De'Longhi Capsule at £59 heats in minutes, but the heat stops as soon as it switches off.

Do you want a timer or app control?

A timer stops you heating an empty room. No. 1 De'Longhi Dragon 4, No. 4 Dimplex ECR20TIE at £99 and No. 7 Pro Breeze OmniWarm at £79 have a 24 hour timer. No. 5 Dimplex EVO2BTA at £179 adds a 7 day timer with Bluetooth app control, No. 9 Dimplex convector at £49 has a 7 day timer without an app, and No. 6 Princess Smart Glass Panel at £99 has a 24 hour timer with Wi-Fi app control. No. 2 Russell Hobbs has no timer.

How large is the room?

Most heaters here are 2 kW, which their makers rate for rooms of about 20 to 25 square metres. For a larger room on a budget, No. 7 Pro Breeze OmniWarm gives 2.5 kW for £79, is rated for up to 30 square metres and comes with a remote control. For under a desk or a very small space, No. 10 Pro Breeze Mini Ceramic Fan Heater at £29 is compact enough and is rated for up to 15 square metres.

Will it stand on the floor or hang on the wall?

For a fixed spot, No. 6 Princess Smart Glass Panel at £99 mounts on the wall or stands on the floor and has a child lock and an IP24 rating, though the glass gets hot. No. 9 Dimplex convector at £49 can also be wall mounted, comes with brackets and weighs 3.8 kg. Oil-filled radiators are heavy, 14.4 kg for No. 1, but have wheels to move them between rooms.

Do you want a heater you can use all year?

When not to buy

  • Any heater because it promises more heat from less electricity. Every plug-in heater here turns its electricity into heat at the same rate: 2 kW costs about 53p an hour at the 26.32p price cap whichever type you buy. Maker claims such as Dyson's 'up to 30%' (a thermostat against running flat out at 2,050 W) or Dimplex's 'up to 18%' (against oil-filled, conditions not given) describe control and warm-up, not a different kind of efficiency.
  • Portable heaters to replace central heating in the whole house. At 4 hours a day, one 2 kW heater on full power costs about £63 a month and three of them about £190. Plug-in heaters pay off when you heat one room and turn the central heating down. De'Longhi and Dyson both state on their own pages that these products are only suitable for well insulated spaces or occasional use.
  • A fan heater for a bedroom or a long evening. Pro Breeze quotes 55 dB for No. 10 and Dyson 63 dBA at maximum for No. 3 (46 dBA in quiet mode), and the heat stops the moment a fan heater switches off. For several hours of quiet heat pick a radiator such as No. 1, No. 2 or No. 4.

Buying guide: how to choose the right energy efficient electric heaters

Are some electric heaters more efficient?

All plug-in electric heaters convert close to 100 percent of their electricity into heat, so one 2 kW heater uses the same energy as another per hour on full power. The real savings come from a precise thermostat, a timer and heating only the room or the person that needs it. A heater that switches off as soon as the room is warm costs less to run than one that keeps going.

Which type suits your room?

Oil-filled radiators are slow to warm up but give steady, silent heat and keep radiating after they switch off, which suits living rooms and bedrooms used for several hours. Oil-free micathermic and convector heaters warm up faster. Fan heaters are best for short bursts in small rooms or for warming yourself at a desk.

What does it cost to run?

At the Ofgem price cap of 26.32p per kWh (1 October to 31 December 2026), a 2 kW heater on full power costs about 53p an hour and a 2.5 kW heater about 66p. On an 800 or 900 W setting, or with the thermostat cycling, it can be less than half that. Heating one room this way is often cheaper than running gas central heating for the whole house, but more expensive per unit of heat than gas.

Thermostat and timer

A digital thermostat that shows degrees is more accurate than a numbered dial and avoids overheating. A 24 hour timer lets you warm a room before you get up or come home without leaving the heater on all day. Smart heaters add app control and weekly schedules.

Safety

Look for an overheat cut-out and a tip-over switch. Most heaters in this list have both, and where the maker does not list a tip-over switch we say so in the review. Keep heaters at least a metre from curtains, furniture and bedding, never cover them, and plug them directly into a wall socket rather than an extension lead, as a 2 kW heater draws a lot of current.

Frequently asked questions

Which are the best electric heaters for energy efficiency in the UK?

Looking for the best electric heaters for energy efficiency, focus on features rather than wattage. Choose a heater with a digital thermostat that keeps the room within a degree, a 24-hour or 7-day timer, and a lower power setting such as 800 W or 1,200 W for mild days. In our ranking the De'Longhi Dragon 4 leads for steady, thermostat-controlled heat over several hours, the Dimplex Eco Oil Free Radiator adds a 7-day timer, and the Princess Smart Glass Panel Heater can be controlled from your phone. Size matters too: a heater that is too small runs constantly, while one that is right for the room reaches temperature and then cycles off. At the current UK electricity price cap, every kWh you avoid wasting saves real money over the winter.

What is the most energy efficient electric heater?

Every plug-in electric heater turns close to 100% of the electricity it uses into heat, so no model creates more heat per kWh than another. What makes one heater cheaper to run is control: an accurate thermostat, a timer, an eco or low-power setting and heating only the room you are using. That is why the De'Longhi Dragon 4 and the Dimplex Eco Oil Free Radiator are our top picks. Both hold a set temperature and switch off once the room is warm, instead of running flat out. Fan heaters are efficient for short bursts in small rooms, while oil-filled and oil-free radiators suit longer evenings. Be wary of any heater sold as using far less power for the same warmth: in the UK that claim is physically impossible.

Is an oil-filled radiator cheaper than a fan heater?

Per kWh they cost the same. An oil-filled radiator is better for heating a room for hours because it holds heat and cycles off. A fan heater is better for short bursts, as it heats quickly and you only use it while you are there.

Is it cheaper to use an electric heater than central heating?

If you only need to heat one room, usually yes. Heating the whole house with gas is cheaper per unit of heat, but heating empty rooms wastes energy. Many households use a portable heater in the living room in the evening and keep the central heating lower.

Can I leave an electric heater on overnight?

Oil-filled radiators with a thermostat and safety cut-out are designed for long use, but it is safer to use a timer. Never leave fan heaters running unattended, and never cover any heater.

What size heater do I need?

Go by the maker's room rating. The 2 kW heaters in this list are rated for about 20 to 25 square metres: Russell Hobbs and Dimplex quote 20 m² and Princess 25 m². Pro Breeze rates its 2.5 kW OmniWarm for up to 30 m² and its mini fan heater for 15 m². Draughty or poorly insulated rooms need more, and De'Longhi and Dyson both state that these heaters are only suitable for well insulated spaces or occasional use.

Are oil-free radiators better than oil-filled radiators?

They suit different use. An oil-free radiator such as the Dimplex ECR20TIE (£99) warms up faster and is lighter to move, so it fits rooms you use for short periods. An oil-filled radiator like the De'Longhi Dragon 4 (£149) is slow to warm up but holds heat after switching off, which suits long evenings. Both have a 24 hour timer.

What is the best electric heater for a bedroom?

A heater without a fan and with a thermostat works best, so pick an oil-filled radiator rather than a fan heater. The Russell Hobbs 2kW Oil Filled Radiator (£55) is quiet and cheap but has no timer. The De'Longhi Dragon 4 (£149) adds a 24 hour timer so it can switch off by itself. Ceramic fan heaters like the Pro Breeze Mini (£29), which Pro Breeze rates at 55 dB, are too noisy for sleeping.

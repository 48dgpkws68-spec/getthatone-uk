Buying guide: how to choose the right TVs under £300

What does £300 buy you?

For up to £300 you can choose between a 43 inch QLED from a big brand, a 50 inch 4K set or even a 55 inch QLED from TCL. All TVs in this list are 4K with HDR and a smart system. The real differences are colour, brightness, HDR formats and software. Decide first whether you want the largest possible screen or a smaller set with a better panel.

Which size suits your room?

A simple rule is that the viewing distance should be about one and a half to two times the screen diagonal. For a 43 inch TV that means roughly 1.6 to 2.2 metres, for 50 inch about 1.9 to 2.5 metres and for 55 inch about 2.1 to 2.8 metres. In a bedroom or kitchen 43 inches is usually enough, in a living room 50 or 55 inches feels more like a cinema.

QLED or standard LED?

QLED sets use a layer of quantum dots that makes colours richer and keeps them saturated at higher brightness. Standard LED sets are cheaper and fine for news, soaps and sport. At this budget no TV has local dimming, so blacks look slightly grey in a dark room. If you watch mainly in the evening, put a lamp behind the TV for a calmer picture.

Check the HDR formats

Netflix and Disney+ use Dolby Vision, Prime Video mostly HDR10+. A TV that supports both, such as the TCL 55P7K-UK, Hisense 43A7QTUK or Amazon Fire TV Omni QLED, always plays the best version. Samsung never supports Dolby Vision. Keep in mind that a cheap TV has limited brightness, so HDR looks better than SDR but never as bright as on a premium set.

Choose the smart system you like

Google TV has the most apps and Chromecast built in. webOS from LG and Tizen from Samsung are fast and tidy. VIDAA from Hisense and Roku TV are simple and quick, and VIDAA includes Freely. Fire TV is handy for Prime Video and Alexa users. Titan OS on Philips is the weakest, but a streaming stick of around £30 fixes that on any TV.

Budget for sound

Slim budget TVs have small speakers. Dolby Atmos or DTS:X on the box does not mean deep bass. If you watch a lot of films or sport, a simple soundbar of £80 to £150 makes a bigger difference than spending more on the TV. Check that the TV has an HDMI ARC or eARC port, which all ten models here do.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best TV under £300?

The TCL 55P7K-UK is the best all round choice: a 55 inch QLED with Dolby Vision, HDR10+ and Google TV for about £299. If you want to spend less, the TCL 50V6C-UK gives you a 50 inch 4K Google TV for around £229.

Can you get a 55 inch TV for under £300?

Yes. The TCL 55P7K-UK has been listed for about £299 and is a QLED with Dolby Vision and Google TV. Prices move around the £300 mark, so check the live price on Amazon.co.uk before you order.

Is a 4K TV under £300 worth it?

Yes, for normal viewing. Every TV in this list is 4K with HDR and has BBC iPlayer, ITVX, Netflix and Disney+. You give up local dimming, high brightness and 120 Hz gaming, which matter mostly for film fans and serious gamers.

Which cheap TV has Freely?

The Hisense 50E6QTUK and Hisense 43A7QTUK have Freely built in, which brings live channels and catch up from BBC, ITV, Channel 4 and Channel 5 over the internet. Other models use Freeview Play or separate catch up apps.

Which TV under £300 is best for gaming?

The TCL 55P7K-UK has a 120Hz Game Accelerator mode and ALLM, which makes it the most suitable for a PS5 or Xbox in this list. All other models run at 60 Hz, which is fine for casual gaming.

Is a 43 inch TV big enough?

For a bedroom, kitchen or small living room where you sit about 2 metres away, 43 inches is a good size. If you sit further away, a 50 inch model such as the TCL 50V6C-UK or Hisense 50E6QTUK costs only a little more.

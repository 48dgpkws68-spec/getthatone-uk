Ticket to Ride Europe from Days of Wonder is the board game we would recommend to most families first. It is for 2 to 5 players aged 8 and up, and a game takes 30 to 60 minutes. On your turn you do one thing: draw train cards, claim a route between two cities or take new destination tickets. Whoever connects the cities on their tickets and builds the longest routes scores the most points.

The Europe edition adds tunnels, which can cost extra cards, ferries that need locomotive cards, and stations that let you use an opponent's route. That keeps it interesting after many plays without making the rules heavy. In the box are a board, 240 trains, 15 stations and 156 cards. It won the International Gamers Award in 2005 and is still one of the best rated family games on Amazon.co.uk. It is a complete game, so you do not need the original Ticket to Ride. Do clear the table, because the map is big.

Kingdomino from Blue Orange is a tile laying game for 2 to 4 players aged 8 and up, and a round takes only about 15 minutes. Each player builds a small kingdom from domino style tiles showing fields, forests, lakes and mines. A new tile has to match a terrain that is already in your kingdom. At the end, each area scores its number of squares multiplied by the crowns in it.

The clever part is how you choose tiles: whoever takes the best tile this round picks last in the next one. That gives children a real decision every turn without long waiting. The game won the Spiel des Jahres award in 2017. At under £20 it is the cheapest game in this guide and the box is small enough to take on holiday. With more than four people you need the Age of Giants expansion, which adds a fifth player. Adding up the final score is a nice bit of maths practice.

Catan is one of the best selling modern board games in the world, and the 6th Edition is the current version on Amazon.co.uk. It is for 3 or 4 players aged 10 and up, and a game takes 60 to 90 minutes. You build roads, settlements and cities on an island made of hexagonal tiles. The dice decide which tiles produce brick, wood, wheat, ore or sheep each turn, and the first player to reach ten victory points wins.

What sets Catan apart is the trading. You almost never have the resources you need, so you bargain with the other players, also when it is not your turn. Because the island is laid out differently every time, no two games are the same. The drawbacks: it does not work with two players, and a run of bad dice rolls can be frustrating for younger children. For 5 or 6 players you need a separate extension, and expansions require this 6th Edition base game.

Carcassonne from Z-Man Games won the Spiel des Jahres in 2001 and has been a family favourite ever since. It is for 2 to 5 players aged 7 and up and takes about 45 minutes. On your turn you draw one tile and add it to the growing landscape of cities, roads, monasteries and fields. Then you may place one of your meeples on it to score points when that feature is finished.

The current edition contains 72 land tiles, 40 meeples in five colours and a scoreboard, and the River and Abbot mini expansions are in the box. Because there is no text on the tiles, younger children can join in easily, and many families start without the farmers, which are the hardest part to score. Buyers on Amazon.co.uk like that it works just as well with two players as with five. Between adults it can become surprisingly sharp, because you can block someone else's city with one well placed tile.

Azul is a tile game for 2 to 4 players aged 8 and up, and a game takes 30 to 45 minutes. In turn you take all tiles of one colour from a display in the middle of the table and place them in a row on your own board. Complete rows move to your wall and score points; tiles you cannot place cost you points. It won the Spiel des Jahres in 2018.

The rules fit on a few pages, but you soon notice that every choice also decides what is left for the next player. That makes it interesting for adults while children of 8 can follow along. The thick, colourful tiles and the cloth bag are a big part of the charm, according to many buyers on Amazon.co.uk. There is not much of a story, so children who love characters and adventures may prefer Labyrinth or Ticket to Ride. Note that Azul Duel is a different game for two players only.

Ravensburger Labyrinth is a moving maze game for 2 to 4 players aged 7 and up, and a game takes 20 to 30 minutes. The board consists of fixed and loose maze tiles. On your turn you slide a spare tile into a row, which shifts the whole row and changes the paths. Then you move your piece as far as the corridors allow, trying to reach the treasure on your next card.

In the box are a game board, 34 maze tiles, 24 treasure cards and 4 playing pieces. Because there is no text to read, it works well with mixed ages, and children often turn out to be better at spotting routes than their parents. It usually costs around £22 to £23 on Amazon.co.uk. There are many themed versions, such as Disney, Minecraft and Pokemon, which play the same way but often cost more. For teenagers the game can feel a bit simple after a while, and it stops at four players.

Pandemic by designer Matt Leacock is a cooperative board game for 2 to 4 players, with an average play time of about 45 minutes. The listing gives ages 8 and up. You do not play against each other but as a team against the game: four diseases spread across the world map and you travel between cities to treat them, build research stations and collect cards to find the cures.

Each player takes a role with its own skill, such as the Medic, the Scientist, the Researcher or the Operations Expert, so everyone has something to contribute. You win together or lose together, which makes it a good choice for families where competitive games end in tears. A known pitfall is that one experienced player starts telling everybody what to do. Agree that each player decides their own turn. The theme of outbreaks is handled in an abstract way with coloured cubes, but it is worth deciding for yourself whether it suits your children.

Cluedo from Hasbro Gaming is the murder mystery that generations of British families grew up with. It is for 2 to 6 players aged 8 and up. Three cards, one suspect, one room and one weapon, are hidden in an envelope at the start. You move through the mansion, make suggestions and note which cards the other players show you, until you can name the right combination.

The current edition has updated characters and artwork, but the idea is unchanged. Its biggest advantage for families is the player count: with six seats it works at Christmas when the modern strategy games in this guide stop at four or five. It also teaches children to reason by ruling things out. The weaker points are well known. You move by rolling dice, so a few low rolls can keep you out of the room you need, and with two players there is little to deduce. At around £20 it is one of the cheaper games here.

Buying guide: how to choose the right board games for families

How many players do you usually have?

Check the player count first. Many modern games stop at four players, and Catan needs at least three. For a family of five or six, Ticket to Ride Europe, Carcassonne and Cluedo are safe choices. If you often play with two, look at Kingdomino, Azul and Carcassonne, which all work well as a pair.

Take the age on the box seriously

The age on the box tells you from when a child can follow the rules and enjoy the game. A game that is too hard ends in frustration, one that is too easy bores older siblings. When in doubt, base your choice on the youngest player. Games for 6+ and 7+ in this guide need no reading during play.

Play time and explanation

On a school night, a game of 15 to 30 minutes gets played far more often than one of 90 minutes. Kingdomino and Labyrinth are quick; Catan and Wingspan need a free afternoon or evening. Also think about how long the rules take to explain: most games here can be taught in under ten minutes.

Against each other or together

In most board games you play against each other. If that often ends in arguments, a cooperative game such as Pandemic is worth trying: the whole family plays as one team against the game. Games with little direct conflict, such as Wingspan and Kingdomino, are also a calmer choice.

Classic or modern board game?

Classics such as Cluedo are known by everyone and need no explanation at a family gathering. Modern games such as Ticket to Ride, Carcassonne and Azul rely less on dice and more on choices, and nobody is knocked out before the end. Many families enjoy having one of each.

What to check when ordering

Many popular games have expansions, junior versions and special editions with similar names. Check that you are ordering the base game in English and not an expansion or a foreign language edition. Frequently asked questions

What is the best board game for families in 2026?

Based on specs and buyer reviews, Ticket to Ride Europe is the best family board game. It is for 2 to 5 players from age 8, takes 30 to 60 minutes and is easy to explain. Kingdomino is the best value at under £20.

Which board game is good for 2 players?

Kingdomino, Azul and Carcassonne all play very well with two. Ticket to Ride Europe also works with two players. Catan needs at least three players, and Cluedo is less fun with two.

Which board game is suitable for young children?

Ticket to Ride First Journey Europe is labelled 6+, and Labyrinth and Carcassonne are labelled 7+. None of the three needs reading during play, so younger children can join in alongside older siblings and adults.

Which family board game is fun for adults too?

Catan, Azul and Carcassonne stay interesting for adults because your choices matter more than luck. Wingspan is the richest game in this guide, but it is labelled 14+ and is best for families with teenagers.

How much does a good family board game cost?

Most good family board games cost between £19 and £35 on Amazon.co.uk. Kingdomino and Cluedo are at the lower end at around £20. Wingspan is the most expensive in this guide at around £48.

Is a board game a good Christmas present?

Yes, a board game is a present the whole family uses together, and most games in this guide last for years. Choose by the youngest player's age and the usual number of players, and order in good time, because popular games can sell out in December.

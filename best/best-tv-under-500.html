Buying guide: how to choose the right TVs under £500

What does £500 buy you?

For under £500 you can choose between a branded 55 inch TV, a 65 inch set from Hisense or TCL, or a 55 inch Mini LED model. All are 4K, all have HDR and all are smart TVs. The bigger differences are in brightness, contrast in a dark room, refresh rate for gaming and the quality of the software. Decide first what matters most to you: size, picture quality or brand.

QLED, QNED and Mini LED explained

QLED and QNED both use a colour layer with quantum dots or similar technology for richer colour than a basic LED TV. Mini LED goes further: thousands of tiny LEDs light the screen in zones, so dark scenes stay darker and highlights get brighter. In a dark room that difference is clearly visible. In a bright living room a good QLED is often enough.

Big brand or budget brand?

LG and Samsung offer polished software and long support, but at this price you get a simpler panel. Hisense and TCL give you more for the same money, such as Mini LED, 144 Hz or 65 inches, with slightly busier menus. Toshiba and Philips sit in between. Read the buyer reviews on Amazon.co.uk to see how a model holds up after a year.

Check the smart platform and Freely

Google TV has the most apps and Chromecast built in. webOS from LG and Tizen from Samsung are fast and tidy. VIDAA from Hisense and Fire TV from Toshiba and Amazon are simpler. Many 2025 and 2026 models include Freely, which puts live channels and catch up from BBC, ITV, Channel 4 and Channel 5 in one guide over the internet. Check that the apps you use daily are supported.

Gaming at 60 or 144 Hz

A PS5 or Xbox Series X can output up to 120 frames per second. You only benefit from that on a TV with a 120 Hz or 144 Hz panel, HDMI 2.1 and VRR, such as the TCL 55T8C-UK or 55C6K-UK. A 60 Hz TV with ALLM still works well for casual gaming, but motion in fast shooters and racing games looks less smooth.

Budget for a soundbar

Slim TVs have little room for speakers. Models with Onkyo sound or a built in subwoofer, such as the TCL and the Hisense 55A7QTUK, sound better than average, but a soundbar of around £100 to £200 makes a much bigger difference to films and sport. Keep that in mind when you choose between a TV at £350 and one at £490.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best TV under £500?

The LG 55QNED82A6B is the best all round choice: a 55 inch QNED from a big brand with the fast webOS system and every UK app. If you want more screen, the Hisense 65E7QTUK is the best value.

Which 65 inch TV is good under £500?

The Hisense 65E7QTUK is the best 65 inch option at this budget. It has a QLED panel, Dolby Vision, Dolby Atmos and Freely built in. Contrast in a dark room is average, but for a bright living room it is excellent value.

Is a cheap TV from a lesser known brand a good idea?

Hisense and TCL are among the largest TV makers in the world and offer good quality for the money. Avoid truly unknown brands without UK reviews. Check the warranty and buyer reviews on Amazon.co.uk before you order.

Can you game on a TV under £500?

Yes. The TCL 55T8C-UK and TCL 55C6K-UK both have a 144 Hz panel with VRR, ideal for a PS5 or Xbox Series X. Most other TVs in this list run at 60 Hz with ALLM, which is fine for casual gaming.

Which cheap smart TV has the best apps?

Google TV on the TCL models has the largest choice of apps and Chromecast built in. LG webOS and Samsung Tizen are fast and include all major UK apps such as BBC iPlayer, ITVX and Channel 4.

Is a TV under £500 still 4K?

Yes, every TV in this list is 4K Ultra HD with HDR. The differences are in brightness, contrast and HDR formats such as Dolby Vision, not in resolution.

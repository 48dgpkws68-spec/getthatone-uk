Buying guide: how to choose the right bathroom heater

Check the IP rating first

The IP rating shows how well a heater resists water. The second number matters most in a bathroom: 2 means protection against dripping water, 4 against splashes from any direction. For a bathroom choose at least IP22, and preferably IP24. Even then, keep the heater out of the zones directly around the bath and shower.

Bathroom zones and plugs

UK wiring rules divide a bathroom into zones. Zone 0 is inside the bath or shower tray, Zone 1 directly above it and Zone 2 within 60 cm. Outside those zones is where IP24 heaters belong. Sockets are not allowed close to the bath or shower, so check where the nearest socket is. For a fixed installation, most manufacturers advise a qualified electrician.

Fan heater or panel heater?

A downflow fan heater gives a quick blast of warm air, ideal for a few minutes before and after a shower, but it makes some noise. A panel heater heats the room more evenly and silently, which suits bathrooms you want warm for longer. Many cheap downflow fan heaters are sold bare wired, so if you need a plug check the listing.

How many watts do you need?

As a rough guide, 500W suits a cloakroom, 1000W a shower room or en suite up to about 12 to 15 m², and 1800 to 2000W a larger family bathroom or one with a cold outside wall. More watts warms faster, but a thermostat stops the heater running at full power once the room is warm.

Timer and app make the difference

In a bathroom you want heat at set times: before you get up and in the evening. A timer or app schedule starts the heater automatically, so you do not waste energy heating an empty room. Open window detection helps when you air the room after a shower.

What does a bathroom heater cost to run?

A 2 kW heater running at full power for an hour uses 2 kWh. At a unit rate of around 25p per kWh that is about 50p an hour. Because the thermostat cycles the heater on and off and you only heat for short periods, the real cost per day is usually much lower.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best heater for a bathroom?

The Futura 2000W glass panel heater is our top pick: IP24, a UK 3 pin plug, a digital thermostat and wall or floor mounting for under £90. For a small bathroom, the Futura 1000W or Devola DVPW1000G are good choices.

Can I use a normal fan heater in the bathroom?

No. A standard fan heater has no protection against water and steam. Use a heater with an IP rating of at least IP22, preferably IP24, and keep it out of the zones around the bath and shower.

What IP rating does a bathroom heater need?

At least IP22 and preferably IP24. IP24 protects against splashes from any direction. These heaters belong outside Zones 0, 1 and 2, which are the areas in and directly around the bath and shower.

Can I plug a heater into a socket in the bathroom?

UK rules do not allow standard sockets close to a bath or shower. Many people use a socket just outside the bathroom or have an electrician wire the heater into a fused spur. Most manufacturers advise a qualified electrician for a fixed bathroom installation.

What is the best fan heater for a bathroom?

The TCP Smart Down Flow Ceramic Heater 2000W is IP22 rated, hangs high on the wall and can be controlled by app, remote or voice. It heats quickly but makes some fan noise.

How much does it cost to run a bathroom heater?

A 2 kW heater uses 2 kWh per hour at full power, about 50p at 25p per kWh. A 1 kW heater costs about half that. With a thermostat and timer the daily cost is usually much lower.

Heated throws go over you on the sofa, while electric blankets sit under your sheet to warm the bed before you get in. Buying guide: how to choose the right heated throws

Heated throw or electric blanket?

A heated throw goes over you, on the sofa, in a chair or at a desk. An electric underblanket sits on the mattress under your sheet and pre-warms the bed before you get in. Some people use a heated throw as an over-blanket on the bed too. If you are mainly cold in the evenings, choose a throw. If you struggle to warm up in bed, an underblanket is better.

How much does it cost to run?

Very little. Most heated throws use 100 to 160 W on full power and much less on lower settings. At around 27p per kWh, that is roughly 1 to 4p an hour. An electric underblanket pre-heating a bed for 30 minutes costs about a penny. Compared with heating a room, the savings over a winter can be significant.

Safety features to look for

Choose a throw or blanket with overheat protection and an automatic shut-off timer. Check it carries the CE or UKCA mark and comes from an established brand. Never fold or bunch an electric blanket while it is on, do not use it with a hot water bottle, and replace any blanket older than ten years or showing worn wires.

Size and dual controls

Standard heated throws are around 160 by 120 cm, enough for one person. For sharing, look for 180 by 130 cm or larger. For electric blankets, match the size to your bed. Couples often prefer a dual control blanket so each person can choose their own temperature.

Washing and care

Almost all modern heated throws and electric blankets are machine washable once you detach the controller, usually on a gentle 30 or 40 degree cycle. Let them dry completely before plugging in again. Avoid tumble drying unless the label says it is allowed.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best heated throw in the UK?

The Silentnight Luxury Heated Throw is the best all-rounder for around £45. For a more luxurious feel, the Dreamland Hygge Days Faux Fur is the premium choice.

Is it cheaper to use a heated throw than the central heating?

Yes, by a large margin. A heated throw costs a few pence an hour, while heating a home with gas costs far more. Many households use a throw in the evening and set the heating lower.

Can I sleep with an electric blanket on?

Only if the blanket is designed for overnight use on its lowest setting, such as the Silentnight Yours & Mine. Otherwise, use it to warm the bed and switch it off when you get in.

Are heated throws safe?

Modern heated throws from established brands are safe when used correctly. They have overheat protection and auto shut-off. Do not use one that is damaged, and do not let pets chew the cable.

Can you wash a heated throw?

Yes. Detach the controller and wash it on a gentle cycle, usually at 30 degrees. Make sure it is completely dry before using it again.

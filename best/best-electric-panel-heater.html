Buying guide: how to choose the right electric panel heaters UK

Panel heater vs oil filled radiator

Both turn nearly all their electricity into heat, so a 2 kW panel heater and a 2 kW oil filled radiator use the same energy per hour at full power. The difference is how they behave. A panel heater is light, slim and warms the air within minutes, but stops giving heat soon after it switches off. An oil filled radiator takes 15 to 20 minutes to get going and keeps radiating afterwards. Choose a panel heater for rooms you heat in short blocks or want wall mounted, and an oil filled radiator for long, steady evenings.

How much does a panel heater cost to run per hour?

Take the wattage in kW and multiply it by your electricity unit rate. A 2 kW panel uses 2 kWh per hour at full power, a 1 kW panel 1 kWh. As an example, at a unit rate of 25p per kWh that is 50p and 25p per hour. Your own rate depends on your tariff and region, so check your bill. In practice the thermostat switches the heater off once the room is warm, so the real cost over an evening is usually lower than the full-power figure.

What wattage do you need for your room?

As a rough guide, allow about 100 W per square metre in a reasonably insulated UK room. That means 1 kW for a box room or small bedroom up to about 10 to 12 square metres, 1.5 to 1.8 kW for 15 to 20 square metres and 2 kW for living rooms of about 20 to 25 square metres. Conservatories, rooms with high ceilings and poorly insulated houses need more.

What is Lot 20 and does it matter?

Lot 20 is the Ecodesign rule for electric room heaters that has applied since 2018. For fixed heaters such as wall panels it requires an electronic thermostat, a 24 hour or seven day timer and at least one smart saving feature, such as open window detection or adaptive start. A Lot 20 compliant panel therefore wastes less than an old on/off convector. It matters most if you fit heaters permanently, for example as a landlord.

Wall mounted or freestanding?

Most panels in this list come with both brackets and feet. Wall mounting saves floor space and looks neater, and is the normal choice for a heater that stays in one room. Feet are handy if you rent or want to move the heater. In a bathroom the heater must be IP24 rated, placed outside the splash zones and installed by a qualified electrician. Plug-in models should go straight into a wall socket, not an extension lead.

Wi-Fi, timer or both?

A timer is the feature that saves the most, because the heater only runs when someone is in the room. A seven day timer on the unit does that without any app. Wi-Fi makes schedules easier to set and lets you switch the heating on from your phone when plans change. Most budget smart heaters only connect on 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi, so check your router settings first.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best electric panel heater in the UK?

For most homes the Devola DVPW2000 (£99) is the best choice, with 2 kW, app schedules, a seven day timer and open window detection. The Purus PCV1800 (£75) is the best value and the Dimplex PLX200E (£189) the best premium wall heater.

How much does a 2kW panel heater cost to run per hour?

At full power a 2 kW panel heater uses 2 kWh per hour. Multiply that by your unit rate: at 25p per kWh, for example, that is 50p per hour. Unit rates differ per tariff and region, so use the rate on your own bill. Once the room is warm the thermostat cuts in and the real cost per hour drops.

Is a panel heater cheaper to run than an oil filled radiator?

Not per kilowatt: both convert almost all electricity into heat, so the same wattage costs the same per hour at full power. A panel heater warms a room faster, which suits short heating periods. An oil filled radiator holds its heat longer, which suits long evenings. A good thermostat and timer make more difference than the type.

What size panel heater do I need?

Allow about 100 W per square metre in a reasonably insulated room. A 1 kW panel such as the TESY CN51 (£97) suits up to about 10 to 12 square metres, the 1.8 kW Purus (£75) is rated for 20 square metres and 2 kW models such as the Devola (£99) or Futura (£87) cover roughly 20 to 25 square metres.

Are electric panel heaters expensive to run?

Per unit of heat, electricity costs more than gas, so heating a whole house with panel heaters is expensive. They are economical when you heat one room for a few hours and leave the central heating off. Use the timer, set the thermostat to around 18 to 20 °C and keep doors closed.

What does Lot 20 compliant mean?

It means the heater meets the Ecodesign rules for electric room heaters in force since 2018. A compliant fixed panel has an electronic thermostat, a 24 hour or seven day timer and a saving feature such as open window detection or adaptive start. The Dimplex PLX200E (£189), Purus PCV1800 (£75) and both MYLEK models are sold as Lot 20 compliant.

Can you leave a panel heater on overnight?

Panel heaters with a thermostat and overheat protection are designed for long periods of use. Keep the heater clear of curtains and bedding, never cover it or dry clothes on it and use the timer or a low temperature setting. A wall mounted panel is the safer option at night because it cannot be knocked over.

Can I use a panel heater in a bathroom?

Only if it is rated IP24 or higher, placed outside the splash zones around the bath or shower and installed by a qualified electrician. The Devola DVPW2000 (£99), TESY CN51 (£97), Purus PCV1800 (£75) and Futura glass panel (£87) are listed as IP24 rated.

Buying guide: how to choose the right robot vacuums for pet hair

Why pets call for a different robot

A dog or cat sheds hair every day, and it collects along skirting boards, under furniture and in rugs. An ordinary robot copes for a while, but hair soon wraps around the brush and suction drops. Pet owners also deal with litter, muddy paw prints and the occasional accident. That makes the brush, obstacle avoidance and the dock more important than headline suction.

Rubber brushes and anti-tangle systems

Bristle rollers catch hair and wind it up. Rubber rollers, split rollers that push hair to the ends and brushes with a built-in comb or cutter keep themselves much cleaner. With a long-haired cat or dog, look for terms such as anti-tangle, zero-tangle or detangling, and check that the side brush is also designed to resist hair.

Obstacle avoidance: the dog mess question

A robot without a camera drives straight through anything in its path, including dog mess, and spreads it across the floor. Models with an AI camera recognise pet waste, cables, socks and bowls and steer around them. If your dog sometimes has accidents indoors, a camera is the single most useful feature to pay for.

Bagged or bagless dock?

A self-emptying dock means you do not have to empty a small bin full of hair after every run. Docks with a sealed bag keep hair and dander inside when you throw it away, which helps with allergies. Bagless docks such as Shark's save on bags but release some dust when emptied. Bags cost roughly £10 to £20 for a few months' supply.

How much suction do you need?

For hard floors and short rugs around 4,000 to 7,000 Pa is enough for pet hair. Thick carpets and rugs where hair works into the pile benefit from 10,000 Pa or more. Suction only helps if the brush stays clear, so a good anti-tangle design matters as much as the number on the box.

Mopping with pets

Muddy paw prints and drool by the water bowl are easier to deal with on a robot that mops. Spinning mops scrub better than a flat pad, and a dock that washes and dries them stops the smell of damp, dirty pads. Make sure the robot lifts its mops on rugs and set a no-go zone around the pet bed.

Frequently asked questions

Which robot vacuum is best for pet hair?

In our comparison the Dreame X40 Ultra Complete comes out on top thanks to its rubber anti-tangle brush, AI camera that avoids pet mess and a dock with a sealed bag. The Roborock Qrevo S Pro is the best value choice.

Can a robot vacuum recognise dog poo?

Only models with an AI camera, such as the Dreame X40 Ultra Complete, Dreame L40s Pro Ultra and Ecovacs Deebot T30 Omni, are designed to recognise and avoid pet waste. Robots without a camera will drive through it.

Will my cat's hair get stuck in the brush?

With a rubber or detangling brush, much less than with a bristle roller. Very long hair can still collect at the brush ends, so check and clean the brush every week or two.

Is a bagged dock better for pet allergies?

Usually yes. A sealed bag traps hair, dander and dust when you throw it away, while emptying a bagless bin releases some of it back into the air. A HEPA filter on the robot also helps.

How often should a robot vacuum run with a dog?

With a dog or cat that sheds, a daily run keeps hair from building up. In moulting season some owners schedule two runs a day in the main living areas.

Will a robot vacuum scare my pet?

Most pets get used to it within a few days. Start runs while you are home, use a quiet mode at first and set a no-go zone around the pet bed and bowls so the robot does not disturb them.

What is the best cheap robot vacuum for pet hair?

The Roborock Q7 Max+ (£249) is the cheapest here, with a rubber main brush and a self-emptying dock, but no camera and a basic mop. The SwitchBot K10+ Pro (£299) has an all-rubber brush and a bag that lasts up to around 90 days. For £399, the Roborock Qrevo S Pro is the best value, with 18,500 Pa and a full washing dock.

How much suction does a robot vacuum need for pet hair?

It depends on your floors. On hard floors and short rugs, lower suction such as 4,200 Pa on the Roborock Q7 Max+ (£249) picks up loose hair. Hair embedded in thicker carpet needs much more, such as 18,500 Pa on the Roborock Qrevo S Pro (£399) or 20,000 Pa on the Dreame X50 (£999). An anti-tangle brush matters as much as suction.

Buying guide: how to choose the right dehumidifiers UK

Compressor or desiccant?

Compressor dehumidifiers cool the air so moisture condenses, like a fridge. They are the most efficient choice for heated homes above about 15 °C. Desiccant models use an absorbent wheel and a small heater, keep working in cold rooms and are lighter and quieter, but use more electricity. In a warm house choose a compressor, in a cold bedroom, garage or caravan a desiccant.

What capacity do you need?

The litres per day figure is measured at 30 °C and 80 percent humidity, so real homes collect less. A 10 to 12 litre model suits a flat or one or two rooms. A 20 litre model handles a three or four bedroom house or a very damp property. Bigger capacity means the machine reaches its target faster and runs less often.

Running costs

Multiply the wattage in kilowatts by the hours of use and your unit rate. A 200 W compressor running eight hours uses 1.6 kWh. Because most models switch off once the target humidity is reached, real use is often lower. Desiccant models use 300 to 700 W, so they cost more in a warm home.

Tank size and drainage

Tanks range from 2 to 6 litres. A small tank in a damp house can mean emptying it twice a day. Almost every model here has a continuous drainage outlet: fit a hose and run it to a sink, shower tray or outside drain so you never have to empty it.

Noise and the bedroom

Compressor models make a low hum of around 38 to 48 dB. If you want to run one at night, look for a sleep or night mode that slows the fan and dims the display. Desiccant models are often quieter, but blow out slightly warm air.

Drying washing indoors

A dehumidifier is one of the cheapest ways to dry clothes indoors. Put it in a small room with the door closed, use laundry mode and place it close to the airer. Combined with a heated airer, most loads dry in a few hours and the moisture does not end up on your walls.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best dehumidifier in the UK?

For most homes the MeacoDry Arete One 20L is the best choice thanks to its low power use, quiet night mode and HEPA filter. The Pro Breeze 20L is the best value and the Ebac 4850 the best premium model.

How much does it cost to run a dehumidifier?

A compressor model of around 200 to 250 W uses about 1.6 to 2 kWh in eight hours. Once the room is dry it rests most of the time, so the daily cost is often well below that. Desiccant models use more power.

Where should I put a dehumidifier?

Place it in the dampest room, often a bedroom, bathroom landing or the room where you dry washing. Keep it a little away from walls and curtains, close the door and windows, and give it a few days to bring the humidity down.

What humidity should my home be?

Between 40 and 60 percent is healthy. Above 60 percent mould and dust mites thrive. Set the target to about 50 percent and the dehumidifier switches on and off by itself.

Can a dehumidifier get rid of mould?

It stops the conditions mould needs by lowering the humidity and preventing condensation. Existing mould still has to be cleaned off with a mould remover. Fix leaks or rising damp first, as a dehumidifier cannot solve structural problems.

Should I leave a dehumidifier on all the time?

With a humidity sensor you can leave it on safely: it stops when the target is reached and starts again when it gets damp. Use continuous drainage if you do not want to empty the tank every day.

Should I buy a desiccant or a compressor dehumidifier?

Choose a compressor model for a heated home: it extracts more water for less power, like the Meaco Arete One (20 litres a day, £230). Choose a desiccant model for a cold garage, caravan or unheated room, where compressors lose capacity. The EcoAir DD3 Classic MK3 (£270) and Meaco DD8L Junior (£150) work in the cold but use more power, around 330 W for the DD8L.

Is a 12 litre dehumidifier big enough?

For a flat, a bedroom or a small bathroom, usually yes. The De'Longhi Tasciugo AriaDry (£170) and BLACK+DECKER 12L (£115) both remove 12 litres a day and are compact and quiet. For a whole house, visible mould or drying washing indoors, a 20 litre model such as the Pro Breeze 20L (£160) or Meaco Arete One (£230) keeps up better.

The 10 best heated clothes airers of 2026

A heated clothes airer dries washing indoors for a fraction of the cost of a tumble dryer. Most models use between 220 and 300 watts, about the same as a few old light bulbs, and warm the rails just enough to speed up drying without damaging fabrics. That makes them popular in UK flats and houses without a garden or a dryer, especially through the damp winter months. We compared the heated airers on Amazon.co.uk on drying space, maximum load, wattage, extras such as covers and timers, and buyer reviews. These are the ten best.

Buying guide: how to choose the right heated clothes airers UK

Is a heated airer cheaper than a tumble dryer?

Yes, by a wide margin. A typical heated airer uses 220 to 300 W, so even running it for six hours uses less than 2 kWh. A tumble dryer cycle often uses 2 to 5 kWh. Multiply the wattage in kilowatts by the hours and your unit rate to see the real cost. The trade-off is time: a heated airer takes several hours, a dryer about one to two.

3-tier, winged or pod?

Three-tier airers offer the most drying space, usually around 21 metres, and suit families. Winged airers are cheaper and lighter with 10 to 12 metres, which suits one or two people. Hot air pods use a fan heater and hangers: they dry faster and reduce creases, but use around 1,000 W. Choose by how much washing you do and how much floor space you have.

Why a cover makes such a difference

Heated rails warm the fabric they touch, but much of the heat escapes into the room. A cover traps that warm air around the laundry and can cut drying times considerably. Some airers include one, for others it costs £10 to £45 extra. Leave a gap or use a cover with vents, so the moisture can escape.

Drying space and load

Drying space is the total length of the rails. A standard 7 to 8 kg washing machine load needs roughly 15 to 20 metres to hang without overlapping. Check the maximum load too: wet washing is heavy, and 10 kg models can struggle with towels and jeans from a full drum.

Tips for faster drying

Use the highest spin speed your clothes allow, spread items out so they do not overlap, and turn thick items halfway. Put the airer in a room with a door you can close, and open a window slightly or run a dehumidifier to deal with the moisture. Never put a heated airer in a bathroom or dry clothes over it while they are dripping wet.

Safety and UK plugs

All models here have a standard UK 3-pin plug and run safely for hours, but plug them straight into a wall socket where possible. Do not cover the rails with blankets or let the cable run under rugs. Most rails reach 45 to 60 °C, warm but not hot enough to burn, so they are suitable for most fabrics. Check the label for items that should not be heated.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best heated clothes airer in the UK?

For most homes the BLACK+DECKER 3-Tier Heated Clothes Airer with Cover is the best choice, thanks to its large drying space, included cover and wheels. The Beldray EH1156 is the best budget option and the Dry:Soon Deluxe 3-Tier the best premium airer.

How much does a heated airer cost to run?

Multiply the wattage in kilowatts by the hours and your electricity unit rate. A 300 W airer running for five hours uses 1.5 kWh. Manufacturers quote roughly 6p to 9p per hour for typical rail airers, while hot air pods cost more because they use around 1,000 W.

How long does it take to dry clothes on a heated airer?

Light clothes usually dry in three to six hours, thick towels and jeans can take longer. A cover, a warm room and a high spin speed shorten the time considerably. Many people simply load it in the evening and take the washing off in the morning.

Can you leave a heated airer on overnight?

Heated airers are designed for long use and the rails only get warm, not hot. A model with a timer, such as the Dry:Soon Deluxe, switches itself off. Keep the cable clear, plug it into a wall socket and make sure the room is ventilated.

Do heated airers cause damp?

The water in your clothes has to go somewhere, so drying indoors adds moisture to the air. Open a window slightly, close the door to the rest of the house or run a dehumidifier nearby to stop condensation and mould.

Is a heated airer worth it?

If you have no tumble dryer or want to cut running costs, yes. It dries washing much faster than an unheated airer in winter and costs far less to run than a dryer. It will not match a dryer for speed, so it suits people who can wait a few hours.

Buying guide: how to choose the right artificial Christmas trees

Which height fits your ceiling?

Measure from floor to ceiling and subtract at least 25 to 30 cm for the stand and the tree topper. With a standard UK ceiling of around 2.4 m, a 6ft (180 cm) or 7ft (210 cm) tree is the safe choice. Only go for 7.5ft or taller if your ceiling is above 2.6 m.

Full or slim?

A full tree of 6 to 7ft is usually 110 to 135 cm wide at the base. Measure the floor space you have, and leave room to walk around it. Pencil and slim trees are 45 to 80 cm wide and fit in hallways, small flats and next to a TV, but hold fewer ornaments.

PVC or PE tips?

PVC tips are flat and cheap and give volume. PE tips are moulded from real branches and look much more natural. The best value trees combine PE tips on the outside with PVC inside. More tips usually means a fuller tree, but compare trees of the same height.

Pre-lit or unlit?

A pre-lit tree saves you wrapping lights every year and spreads them evenly. Buy one from a UK seller with a UK plug or UK transformer and check warm white versus cool white. An unlit tree lets you choose your own lights and lasts longer, because a failed light set does not ruin the tree. Plan on about 100 LEDs per 30 cm of height.

Hinged branches or hook-in?

On hinged trees the branches are fixed and fold down when you assemble the sections, which takes a few minutes. Hook-in trees have separate branches you slot in by colour code. They take longer but are often easier to store. Almost all trees in this list are hinged.

Storage and lifespan

Keep your tree in a sturdy storage bag or the original box in a dry loft or cupboard. Fold the branches up and avoid crushing the tips. A good artificial tree lasts 10 years or more. Most models need 20 to 40 minutes of fluffing each year to look their best.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best artificial Christmas tree in 2026?

The Dellonda DH45 6ft is the best all-rounder for around £78, thanks to its realistic PE tips and full shape. The COSTWAY 6ft is the best value at about £50, and the WeRChristmas Edwardian Spruce is the best pre-lit premium tree.

What size artificial Christmas tree should I buy?

For most UK homes with a ceiling of about 2.4 m, a 6ft or 7ft tree is right. Leave at least 25 cm between the top of the tree and the ceiling for the topper, and check the base width against your floor space.

Is an artificial tree better for the environment than a real one?

Only if you keep it for a long time. Studies suggest an artificial tree needs to be used for roughly 10 years or more to beat a real tree that is recycled or replanted. Buy a sturdy tree and keep it for many Christmases.

How many lights do I need for my tree?

A common rule is about 100 LEDs for every 30 cm of tree height. That means around 600 LEDs for a well lit 6ft tree, or 300 to 400 for a softer look. Slim trees need about half.

How do I make my artificial tree look fuller?

Fluff every tip, starting at the bottom and working from the trunk outwards. Spread tips up, down and sideways to fill gaps. Adding garlands, picks or extra lights also makes a tree look denser.

Are pre-lit trees safe in the UK?

Yes, if you buy from an established seller with a UK plug or UK transformer and the UKCA or CE mark. Avoid trees that only come with a foreign plug and adapter, and switch the lights off when you go to bed or leave the house.

What is the best Christmas tree for a small flat?

A pencil tree takes up the least floor space. The CHRISTOW Snowy Pencil 6ft at £45 is only about 45 cm wide and light to store. If you have a high ceiling, the HOMCOM 7FT Prelit Pencil at £95 is 75 cm wide and comes with 350 warm white LEDs and red berries.

Can I get a pre-lit tree with coloured lights?

Yes. The Dawsons Living 7FT Colour Select at £59 lets you switch between warm white and multicolour LEDs, and it has a UK plug with a 5 m cable. The other pre-lit trees in this list have warm white LEDs, apart from the WeRChristmas Edwardian Spruce at £215, which uses cool white.

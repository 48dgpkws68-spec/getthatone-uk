Buying guide: how to choose the right electric fireplaces

Stove, wall fire or inset?

A freestanding stove stands on the floor or a hearth and plugs straight in. A wall mounted or recessed fire hangs on the wall or sits in a media wall, often under a TV. An inset fire fits into an existing fireplace opening. Choose by where it will go, not only by looks.

How much heat do you need?

Most electric fires offer 1 and 2 kW. As a rule of thumb, 1 kW warms about 10 m² of a well insulated room. A 2 kW stove is fine for an average living room as extra heat, but an electric fire is usually a top up, not your main heating. Wall fires often stop at 1.5 to 1.8 kW.

LED or water vapour flames?

LED effects such as Dimplex Optiflame are cheap to run and work for years. Water vapour fires such as Dimplex Optimyst create 3D flames and smoke that look far more real, but cost more, need refilling and a replacement transducer now and then. Cheap multi colour LED fires look more digital.

Running costs

The heater is what costs money: 2 kW for one hour uses 2 kWh, so at a unit rate of around 25p that is roughly 50p an hour. The flame on its own uses only a few watts up to about 10 W, so a fire with flame only mode can stay on in the evening for pennies.

Controls and safety

A thermostat keeps the room at a steady temperature instead of heating non stop. A remote and timer make daily use easier. Look for overheat protection, a UK plug and the UKCA or CE mark. Never cover the fire, keep it away from curtains and plug it straight into the wall, not into an extension lead.

Measure before you buy

Check width, height and depth against your space. Stoves are usually 45 to 60 cm wide. A 50 inch wall fire is about 127 cm wide and needs a sturdy wall or a recess of the right depth. For a media wall, follow the maker's recess sizes and ventilation advice.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best electric fireplace in 2026?

The Dimplex Club Optiflame is the best all rounder for around £250, with a realistic log flame, remote and thermostat. The Warmlite Stirling is the best value at about £70, and the Dimplex Bayport Optimyst is the premium pick for the most realistic flames.

How much does an electric fire cost to run?

On full heat a 2 kW fire uses 2 kWh per hour, roughly 50p at a unit rate of about 25p. On 1 kW it is about half that. With the heater off, the flame effect uses only a few watts, which costs well under 1p an hour.

Can an electric fire heat a whole room?

A 2 kW fire can warm an average living room, but it costs more per hour than gas central heating. Most people use an electric fire as extra heat in one room, or for the look with the heater off.

Do electric fires need installing?

Freestanding stoves just plug into a UK socket. Wall mounted fires need fixing to a solid wall or stud frame, and recessed or media wall fires need a recess built to the maker's sizes. Hardwired models should be connected by an electrician.

Are electric fires safe?

Yes, when used as the manual says. Choose a fire with overheat protection and a UK plug, keep it clear of curtains and furniture, plug it directly into the wall and switch it off when you go to bed or leave the house.

What is an Opti-myst or Optimyst fire?

Optimyst is a Dimplex technology that mixes fine water vapour with light to create flames and smoke that look 3D. It looks more like a real fire than LED, but you need to refill the water tank and replace the transducer from time to time.

Can I use an electric fire just for the flame effect?

Yes, on most models. The Dimplex Club Optiflame (£248), Dimplex Tango (£180), Dimplex Bayport Optimyst (£450) and Ezee Glow Zara 50 inch (£300) let you run the flame without the heater, which is handy in spring and autumn. On the INMOZATA 50 inch (£280) the flame alone uses only a few watts.

What is the cheapest electric fire worth buying?

The Warmlite Stirling at £70 is the lowest priced stove here with 1 or 2 kW heat and an adjustable thermostat. The VonHaus Electric Log Burner also costs £70 but gives 1.8 kW and suits rooms up to about 17 m². For flames visible from three sides, the HOMCOM at £75 adds stepless temperature control. None of the three has a remote.

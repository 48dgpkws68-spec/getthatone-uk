The 10 best bean to cup coffee machines of 2026

A bean to cup coffee machine grinds fresh beans and makes an espresso, flat white or latte at the touch of a button. It costs more upfront than a pod machine, but each cup costs around 20 to 30p instead of 40 to 60p, so it pays for itself if you drink coffee every day. The main differences are the milk system, the number of drinks, how easy it is to clean and the build quality. We compared the bean to cup machines on Amazon.co.uk on those points, on price and on UK buyer reviews.

Buying guide: how to choose the right best bean to cup coffee machine UK

Automatic milk or steam wand?

If you mostly drink cappuccinos, lattes and flat whites, an automatic milk system such as De'Longhi LatteCrema or Philips LatteGo makes them at the touch of a button. A manual steam wand is cheaper and can make better microfoam with practice, but takes more time. Espresso and americano drinkers do not need an automatic milk system at all.

How much does a cup cost?

With good beans at around £15 to £20 per kilo, a bean to cup machine makes a coffee for about 20 to 30p, plus a few pence of milk. Pod machines cost 40 to 60p per cup. If you drink two coffees a day, a £400 machine can pay for itself within two years compared with pods, and much faster compared with a coffee shop.

Cleaning and maintenance

All bean to cup machines need regular cleaning. Check that the brew unit is removable for rinsing. Milk systems need rinsing after each use, and the simplest designs have no tubes. Descale every few months, or use a water filter to reduce how often you need to. In hard water areas of the UK, a filter is strongly recommended.

Grinder and beans

Most machines use a conical burr grinder of steel or ceramic. More grind settings give you more control. Use fresh, medium to dark roast beans and avoid oily beans, which can clog the grinder. A bypass chute for pre-ground coffee is handy if you want to make the occasional decaf.

Size and space

Bean to cup machines are larger than pod machines, typically 24 to 26 cm wide and 35 to 45 cm deep. Check the height under your wall cupboards, as you need space to fill the bean hopper from the top. Compact models such as the Melitta Latticia OT and Gaggia Brera suit smaller kitchens.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best bean to cup coffee machine in the UK?

The De'Longhi Magnifica Evo with LatteCrema is the best all-rounder for around £399. On a budget, the De'Longhi Magnifica S at about £249 is a proven choice, and the Jura E8 is the premium pick.

Is a bean to cup machine worth it?

If you drink at least one or two coffees a day, yes. The coffee is fresher than from pods and costs less per cup, so the machine pays for itself over time.

Which bean to cup machine is easiest to clean?

The Philips LatteGo machines have the easiest milk system, with just two parts and no tubes. De'Longhi machines are also straightforward, with a removable brew unit and an automatic rinse.

Can I use pre-ground coffee in a bean to cup machine?

Many models, including the De'Longhi Magnifica range, have a separate chute for pre-ground coffee, which is useful for decaf. It is not designed to replace beans for everyday use.

How often should I descale my coffee machine?

Most machines remind you when it is time. In hard water areas of the UK, every one to two months is typical, less often if you use a water filter.

Dobble is the stocking filler that suits almost every household. The tin holds 55 round cards, each with eight symbols, and any two cards always share exactly one symbol. Whoever spots and shouts the match first wins the card. That is the whole idea, so you can explain it in under a minute and start playing straight away. Asmodee puts the age at 6 and up, with 2 to 8 players and rounds of about 10 to 15 minutes.

Buyers on Amazon.co.uk like that children really can beat adults, because it comes down to quick eyes and not knowledge. The tin includes five mini games, so there is some variety once the basic version gets too familiar. The round tin is small enough to push into the toe of a stocking and tough enough for a coat pocket or holiday bag. Do check the listing before you order, because Amazon also sells foreign language and themed editions. The symbols are the same, but the rules leaflet may not be in English. It usually costs around £10.

UNO needs little introduction, and that is exactly why it works as a stocking filler. The standard deck has 108 cards, including the Wild and Wild Draw Four cards, and Mattel gives it an age of 7 and up for 2 to 10 players.

Because up to ten people can join in, it suits Christmas Day when the whole family is around the table, and a round rarely takes long. Buyers on Amazon.co.uk mainly mention how much play they get for the money, although the thin cardboard box tends to wear out, so a rubber band or small tin helps. At around £7 it is the best value in this list. Amazon also sells versions such as UNO House Rules and UNO Teams, so make sure you pick the classic deck if that is what you want.

Bananagrams is a word game without a board. Everyone takes a pile of letter tiles and races to build their own crossword style grid. Nobody waits for a turn, because all players build at the same time, and the first to use up their letters wins the round. The classic version has 144 tiles in a zipped fabric pouch shaped like a banana. The maker gives an age of 7 and up, and a round takes about 15 minutes.

Buyers describe it as Scrabble without the waiting, and many take it on holiday because there is no board to carry. The pouch is a bit longer than a pack of cards, but it still slides into a stocking and looks good enough to give unwrapped. Younger children can find the pace hard next to adults, so some families give them a head start or play in teams. For early readers there is a separate My First Bananagrams from age 4. The classic game usually costs around £13 on Amazon.co.uk.

Buying guide: how to choose the right stocking fillers

Check the size first

A stocking is smaller than you think. Card games in a tin or small box, such as Dobble, UNO and Monopoly Deal, always fit. The Bananagrams pouch and the Rubik's Cube take up more room but still slide in. A handy rule: one bigger item, a few small ones and something sweet in the toe.

Follow the age on the box

For games and toys we only list products where the maker states an age. Dobble is 6 and up, UNO, Bananagrams and Exploding Kittens 7 and up, the Rubik's Cube and Monopoly Deal 8 and up. Small parts are a choking risk for children under 3, so keep these out of a toddler's stocking.

Stocking fillers for adults

Adults get a stocking too in many families. Go for something that gets used up or used daily: the L'OCCITANE hand cream, the Burt's Bees lip balm set or the Victorinox Classic SD on a keyring. The Victorinox has a blade, so it is for adults only.

Set a budget per stocking

Small gifts add up quickly. With four stockings and five items each, you buy twenty presents. Pick a budget per stocking, for example £20 to £30, and mix one gift of around £10 with a few items under £7. UNO, Monopoly Deal, the Burt's Bees set and Terry's Chocolate Orange all stay under that.

Choose things that get used

The best stocking fillers are played with, eaten or used up. A card game comes out again at Easter and on holiday, hand cream and lip balm are finished by spring and chocolate is gone by Boxing Day. Novelty gadgets are fun for five minutes and then become clutter.

Order in time and check the listing

Amazon.co.uk is busy in December, so order before mid December and check the delivery date on the product page. Many games are also listed in foreign language or themed editions, so read the title carefully. Prices in this guide are typical prices from early November and can change daily.

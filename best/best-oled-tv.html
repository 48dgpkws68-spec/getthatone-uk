Buying guide: how to choose the right OLED TVs

OLED, OLED evo or QD-OLED?

All three switch off individual pixels, so black is truly black. A standard OLED panel, as in the LG B5, is the least bright and suits evening viewing. OLED evo panels, as in the LG C5 and G5, add brightness for daytime use. QD-OLED, used by Samsung and Sony, adds a quantum dot layer for more saturated colour at high brightness. In a dim room the differences are small. In a bright room they are easy to see.

How bright does it need to be?

OLED has always been dimmer than Mini LED, but the latest flagships have closed much of the gap. If you mainly watch in the evening, an entry-level model is plenty. If the TV faces a window, pay for a brighter panel such as the LG G5, or choose the Samsung S95F with its matte Glare Free screen. Closing a blind makes a bigger difference than any spec.

HDR formats: Dolby Vision or HDR10+

Every OLED here handles HDR10 and HLG, which BBC iPlayer uses for its UHD programmes. Netflix and Disney+ use Dolby Vision for most HDR titles, while Prime Video leans on HDR10+. LG and Sony support Dolby Vision but not HDR10+. Samsung supports HDR10+ but not Dolby Vision. Philips supports both. If you stream a lot of films on Netflix, Dolby Vision is the one to have.

Gaming: HDMI 2.1 ports and refresh rate

A PS5 or Xbox Series X outputs 4K at up to 120 frames per second, and needs an HDMI 2.1 port to do so. LG and Samsung fit four of them, Sony and Philips two, and one of those is usually the eARC port for a soundbar. Refresh rates of 144 or 165 Hz only matter if you connect a gaming PC. VRR and ALLM are standard on every model in this list.

Burn-in and lifespan

Burn-in is the lasting shadow of a static image, such as a channel logo or a game HUD. Modern OLEDs shift pixels slightly and run short refresh cycles in standby to prevent it, and with normal mixed viewing it is rare. Leave the TV plugged in at the wall so those cycles can run. If one news channel is on for many hours every day, a Mini LED TV is the safer choice.

Buying around Black Friday

OLED prices fall most in November and again around Boxing Day, and last year's models drop furthest once their successors are out. A 2025 OLED at a 2026 price is usually the smartest buy, because year-on-year gains are modest. Check the exact model number before ordering, as bundles with soundbars and imported versions sit alongside the UK models on Amazon.co.uk. Larger sizes of the same series often get the bigger discounts.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best OLED TV in the UK right now?

For most people it is the LG C5 (OLED55C54LA) at around £1,050: excellent contrast, four HDMI 2.1 ports at 144 Hz and all UK apps. The LG B5 at around £750 is the best value, and the Sony Bravia 8 II is the premium pick.

Is an OLED TV worth the extra money?

If you watch films, dramas or sport in the evening, yes. OLED gives perfect blacks and wide viewing angles that an LED or QLED TV cannot match. In a very bright room, choose a brighter OLED such as the LG G5 or Samsung S95F, or consider Mini LED.

Do OLED TVs still suffer from burn-in?

It is rare with normal mixed viewing. Current OLEDs use pixel shifting and automatic refresh cycles to prevent it. The risk rises if the same static logo or game HUD is on screen for many hours every day.

Which OLED TV is best for a PS5 or Xbox?

The LG C5, LG G5, Samsung S90F and Samsung S95F all have four HDMI 2.1 ports with 4K at 120 Hz or more, VRR and low input lag. On a tighter budget the LG B5 also has four HDMI 2.1 ports at 120 Hz.

What is the difference between OLED and QD-OLED?

Both light each pixel individually. QD-OLED adds a quantum dot layer, which gives more saturated colour at high brightness. Samsung's S90F and S95F and the Sony Bravia 8 II use it. LG uses its own OLED evo panels, which are just as strong on contrast.

When is the best time to buy an OLED TV?

Black Friday in late November and the Boxing Day sales are usually the cheapest moments of the year. Models from the previous year, such as the LG C5 and Samsung S90F, tend to see the biggest price drops.
